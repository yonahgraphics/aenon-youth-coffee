Why this work matters

Coffee is Uganda's second export. Youth barely see the profit.

Over 1.7 million Ugandan households grow coffee, yet most young people leave farming behind — the margins are thin and the ladder up feels missing. Aenon rebuilds that ladder: agronomy training, shared processing equipment, and direct buyer relationships that let youth capture value past the farm gate.

We work with existing family land and district cooperatives, so nobody needs to own acreage to start — just time, curiosity, and a plot to learn on.

What we run

Four programs, one goal: coffee as a real career.

Each program is built around a stage of a young person's journey into the coffee trade — from first seedling to first export contract.

🌱

Young Growers Academy

A 9-month field school teaching agronomy, soil health, and organic pest control to farmers aged 15–24.

The five stages every trainee lives through

This is the actual sequence a cherry — and a young farmer's skillset — moves through each season. Scroll to follow it.

🌱
Stage 1

Planting & nursery

Seedlings raised in shaded nurseries for 8–10 months before transplant.

🌼
Stage 2

Flowering

White blossoms signal the start of a roughly 9-month fruiting cycle.

🍒
Stage 3

Ripening & harvest

Cherries are hand-picked only when fully red — the step quality is won or lost.

☀️
Stage 4

Processing & drying

Pulping, fermenting, and sun-drying on raised beds at the shared station.

📦
Stage 5

Grading & sale

Graded green beans are sold through the cooperative to roasters and exporters.
